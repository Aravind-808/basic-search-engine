TITLE: Depression and Anxiety Disorders - Clinical Overview

OVERVIEW
Depression and anxiety disorders are among the most prevalent mental health
conditions worldwide, often co-occurring and significantly impacting quality
of life and functioning.

MAJOR DEPRESSIVE DISORDER
Major depressive disorder (MDD) is characterized by persistent low mood,
anhedonia, changes in sleep and appetite, fatigue, feelings of worthlessness
or guilt, difficulty concentrating, and in severe cases, suicidal ideation,
lasting at least two weeks and causing functional impairment.

ANXIETY DISORDERS
Generalized anxiety disorder involves excessive, uncontrollable worry about
multiple domains lasting six months or more, accompanied by restlessness,
fatigue, muscle tension, and sleep disturbance. Panic disorder involves
recurrent unexpected panic attacks with intense fear and physical symptoms
such as palpitations and shortness of breath.

SCREENING TOOLS
Common validated screening instruments include the PHQ-9 for depression and
the GAD-7 for generalized anxiety, both used in primary care settings to
assess symptom severity.

TREATMENT APPROACHES
Evidence-based treatment includes psychotherapy, particularly cognitive
behavioral therapy (CBT), and pharmacotherapy with selective serotonin
reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) or serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors
(SNRIs) as first-line agents. Combination therapy is often more effective
than either modality alone for moderate to severe presentations.

RISK ASSESSMENT
Clinicians routinely assess for suicidal ideation, intent, and access to
means, as depression significantly increases suicide risk. Safety planning
and appropriate level-of-care determination are essential components of
management.

CHRONIC MANAGEMENT
Ongoing follow-up, medication adherence support, and relapse prevention
strategies are important given the recurrent nature of these conditions.

TITLE: Common Skin Conditions - Dermatology Overview

OVERVIEW
Dermatologic conditions represent a broad category of disorders affecting
the skin, hair, and nails, ranging from benign and self-limited to chronic
and systemic in nature.

ACNE VULGARIS
Acne results from follicular hyperkeratinization, increased sebum
production, Cutibacterium acnes proliferation, and inflammation. Treatment
options include topical retinoids, benzoyl peroxide, topical or oral
antibiotics, and oral isotretinoin for severe nodulocystic cases.

ATOPIC DERMATITIS
Atopic dermatitis (eczema) is a chronic, relapsing inflammatory skin
condition often associated with a personal or family history of atopy,
presenting with pruritic, erythematous, and sometimes lichenified plaques.
Management includes emollients, topical corticosteroids, topical calcineurin
inhibitors, and in severe cases, systemic immunomodulators or biologics.

PSORIASIS
Psoriasis is an immune-mediated condition characterized by well-demarcated,
erythematous plaques with silvery scale, most commonly on extensor
surfaces. Treatment ranges from topical corticosteroids and vitamin D
analogs to phototherapy and systemic biologic agents targeting TNF-alpha,
IL-17, or IL-23 pathways.

CONTACT DERMATITIS
Contact dermatitis may be irritant or allergic in nature, resulting from
direct skin exposure to offending substances, presenting with erythema,
vesicles, and pruritus at the site of contact.

SKIN CANCER
Basal cell carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, and melanoma are the three
most common skin cancers. The ABCDE criteria (Asymmetry, Border
irregularity, Color variation, Diameter >6mm, Evolving) assist in
identifying concerning pigmented lesions warranting biopsy.

FUNGAL INFECTIONS
Superficial fungal infections such as tinea corporis, tinea pedis, and
onychomycosis are treated with topical or oral antifungal agents depending
on severity and extent.

GENERAL PRINCIPLES
Skin biopsy remains a key diagnostic tool for uncertain or atypical
presentations, guiding definitive diagnosis and treatment planning.

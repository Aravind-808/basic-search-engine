TITLE: Anatomy of the Respiratory System

OVERVIEW
The respiratory system facilitates gas exchange, delivering oxygen to the
bloodstream and removing carbon dioxide, while also playing roles in
acid-base balance, vocalization, and immune defense.

UPPER RESPIRATORY TRACT
The upper respiratory tract includes the nasal cavity, pharynx, and larynx.
Air is filtered, humidified, and warmed as it passes through the nasal
passages before reaching the lower airways.

LOWER RESPIRATORY TRACT
The trachea branches into the right and left main bronchi, which further
divide into lobar and segmental bronchi, then bronchioles, terminating in
alveolar sacs where gas exchange occurs across the thin alveolar-capillary
membrane.

LUNGS
The right lung has three lobes (upper, middle, lower) while the left lung
has two lobes (upper, lower) to accommodate space for the heart. Each lung
is enclosed by pleura, a double-layered membrane that reduces friction
during breathing.

MECHANICS OF BREATHING
Inspiration is driven primarily by contraction of the diaphragm and external
intercostal muscles, expanding the thoracic cavity and decreasing
intrapleural pressure. Expiration is largely passive at rest, relying on
elastic recoil of lung tissue.

GAS EXCHANGE
Oxygen diffuses from alveoli into pulmonary capillaries, binding to
hemoglobin in red blood cells, while carbon dioxide diffuses in the opposite
direction to be exhaled.

REGULATION OF BREATHING
Respiratory rate and depth are regulated by the medulla oblongata and pons
in response to chemoreceptor input, primarily sensitive to arterial carbon
dioxide and pH levels.

CLINICAL RELEVANCE
Knowledge of respiratory anatomy underlies diagnosis and management of
conditions such as pneumonia, pneumothorax, chronic obstructive pulmonary
disease, and pulmonary embolism.

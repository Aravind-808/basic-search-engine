TITLE: Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus - Clinical Overview

OVERVIEW
Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) is a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by
insulin resistance and relative insulin deficiency, resulting in hyperglycemia.
It accounts for roughly 90-95 percent of all diagnosed diabetes cases worldwide
and is strongly associated with obesity, sedentary lifestyle, and genetic
predisposition.

EPIDEMIOLOGY
Prevalence has risen sharply over the past three decades, correlating with
increased rates of obesity and urbanization. Risk increases with age, family
history, and certain ethnic backgrounds including South Asian, Hispanic, and
African American populations.

PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
The disease develops through a combination of peripheral insulin resistance in
skeletal muscle, liver, and adipose tissue, along with progressive beta-cell
dysfunction in the pancreas. Chronic hyperglycemia leads to glucotoxicity,
further impairing insulin secretion and worsening resistance in a feed-forward
cycle.

SYMPTOMS
Common presenting symptoms include polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia,
unexplained weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, and slow-healing wounds.
Many patients are asymptomatic at diagnosis and identified through routine
screening.

DIAGNOSIS
Diagnostic criteria include fasting plasma glucose >= 126 mg/dL, 2-hour plasma
glucose >= 200 mg/dL during an oral glucose tolerance test, hemoglobin A1c >=
6.5 percent, or random plasma glucose >= 200 mg/dL with classic symptoms.

TREATMENT
First-line management includes lifestyle modification: dietary changes,
weight loss, and regular physical activity. Metformin remains the standard
first-line pharmacologic agent. Additional drug classes include sulfonylureas,
DPP-4 inhibitors, SGLT2 inhibitors, GLP-1 receptor agonists, thiazolidinediones,
and insulin therapy for advanced disease.

COMPLICATIONS
Long-term complications include diabetic retinopathy, nephropathy, peripheral
neuropathy, cardiovascular disease, and increased risk of infection. Regular
monitoring of HbA1c, lipid panels, renal function, and annual eye examinations
are recommended.

MONITORING
Target HbA1c is generally below 7 percent for most non-pregnant adults,
though targets are individualized based on age, comorbidities, and
hypoglycemia risk.

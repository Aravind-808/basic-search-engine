TITLE: Common Cold Versus Influenza - Differential Comparison

OVERVIEW
Both the common cold and influenza are viral respiratory illnesses, but they
differ significantly in causative agents, severity, and clinical course.

CAUSATIVE AGENTS
The common cold is most often caused by rhinoviruses, though coronaviruses,
adenoviruses, and respiratory syncytial virus can also be responsible.
Influenza is caused by influenza A and B viruses, which undergo frequent
antigenic changes.

ONSET AND SYMPTOMS
Cold symptoms typically develop gradually and include rhinorrhea, nasal
congestion, sore throat, and mild cough, with low-grade or absent fever.
Influenza has an abrupt onset marked by high fever, chills, myalgia, severe
fatigue, headache, and dry cough. Gastrointestinal symptoms are more common
in children with influenza.

DURATION
Cold symptoms generally resolve within 7-10 days. Influenza symptoms may
last 1-2 weeks, with fatigue sometimes persisting longer.

COMPLICATIONS
Colds rarely cause serious complications beyond secondary sinusitis or otitis
media. Influenza can lead to pneumonia, myocarditis, encephalitis, and
exacerbation of chronic conditions, particularly in the elderly, young
children, pregnant women, and immunocompromised individuals.

DIAGNOSIS
Diagnosis is largely clinical. Rapid influenza diagnostic tests (RIDTs) or
PCR-based assays can confirm influenza, especially during outbreak seasons.

TREATMENT
Cold treatment is supportive: rest, fluids, analgesics, and decongestants.
Influenza may be treated with antiviral agents such as oseltamivir when
initiated within 48 hours of symptom onset, particularly in high-risk
patients.

PREVENTION
Annual influenza vaccination is recommended for most individuals over 6
months of age. Hand hygiene and respiratory etiquette reduce transmission of
both illnesses.

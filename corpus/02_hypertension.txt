TITLE: Essential Hypertension - Diagnosis and Management

OVERVIEW
Hypertension, or persistently elevated arterial blood pressure, is one of the
most common chronic conditions and a leading modifiable risk factor for
cardiovascular disease, stroke, and chronic kidney disease.

CLASSIFICATION
Blood pressure is classified as normal (<120/80 mmHg), elevated (120-129/<80),
stage 1 hypertension (130-139/80-89), and stage 2 hypertension (>=140/90).
Hypertensive crisis is defined as systolic pressure >180 mmHg or diastolic
>120 mmHg.

ETIOLOGY
Most cases are classified as essential (primary) hypertension with no single
identifiable cause, though genetics, high sodium intake, obesity, alcohol use,
and physical inactivity contribute. Secondary hypertension may result from
renal artery stenosis, primary aldosteronism, pheochromocytoma, or
obstructive sleep apnea.

CLINICAL PRESENTATION
Hypertension is often asymptomatic, earning it the label "silent killer."
Severe elevations may cause headache, dizziness, epistaxis, or visual
disturbances.

DIAGNOSTIC WORKUP
Diagnosis requires multiple elevated readings on separate occasions, ideally
confirmed with home or ambulatory blood pressure monitoring. Initial workup
includes basic metabolic panel, lipid profile, urinalysis, electrocardiogram,
and assessment for end-organ damage.

MANAGEMENT
Lifestyle interventions include the DASH diet, sodium restriction, weight
loss, regular aerobic exercise, and alcohol moderation. Pharmacologic
treatment options include thiazide diuretics, ACE inhibitors, angiotensin
receptor blockers, calcium channel blockers, and beta-blockers, chosen based
on comorbidities and patient-specific factors.

COMPLICATIONS
Uncontrolled hypertension increases risk of myocardial infarction, heart
failure, stroke, aortic aneurysm, chronic kidney disease, and hypertensive
retinopathy.

FOLLOW UP
Regular blood pressure monitoring and medication adherence are essential for
long-term cardiovascular risk reduction.

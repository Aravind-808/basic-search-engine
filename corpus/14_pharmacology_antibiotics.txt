TITLE: Classes of Antibiotics - Mechanisms and Spectrum

OVERVIEW
Antibiotics are classified based on their mechanism of action and spectrum
of activity against bacterial pathogens, guiding selection for specific
infections.

BETA-LACTAMS
This class includes penicillins, cephalosporins, and carbapenems, which
inhibit bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding penicillin-binding
proteins. Penicillins are effective against many gram-positive organisms,
while cephalosporins offer expanding gram-negative coverage across
generations. Carbapenems provide broad-spectrum coverage reserved for
serious infections.

MACROLIDES
Macrolides such as azithromycin and clarithromycin inhibit bacterial protein
synthesis by binding the 50S ribosomal subunit, commonly used for atypical
respiratory pathogens.

FLUOROQUINOLONES
Fluoroquinolones like ciprofloxacin and levofloxacin inhibit bacterial DNA
gyrase and topoisomerase IV, providing broad-spectrum coverage but carrying
risks of tendon rupture and QT prolongation.

TETRACYCLINES
Tetracyclines inhibit protein synthesis by binding the 30S ribosomal
subunit and are used for atypical infections, acne, and certain zoonotic
diseases, though contraindicated in pregnancy and young children due to
effects on bone and teeth development.

AMINOGLYCOSIDES
Aminoglycosides such as gentamicin bind the 30S ribosomal subunit and are
used for serious gram-negative infections, requiring monitoring due to
nephrotoxicity and ototoxicity risk.

GLYCOPEPTIDES
Vancomycin inhibits cell wall synthesis and is a key agent against
methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) and other resistant
gram-positive organisms.

SULFONAMIDES
Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole inhibits folate synthesis pathways and is
commonly used for urinary tract infections and certain opportunistic
infections.

SELECTION PRINCIPLES
Antibiotic selection should be guided by suspected or confirmed pathogen,
local resistance patterns, site of infection, and patient-specific factors
including allergies and renal function.

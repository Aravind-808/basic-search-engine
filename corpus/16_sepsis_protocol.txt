TITLE: Sepsis Recognition and Management Protocol

OVERVIEW
Sepsis is a life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host
response to infection, representing a leading cause of in-hospital mortality.

DEFINITIONS
Sepsis is defined by evidence of infection plus an increase in Sequential
Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score of two or more points. Septic shock is
a subset of sepsis with circulatory and cellular metabolism abnormalities
severe enough to substantially increase mortality, characterized by
persistent hypotension requiring vasopressors and elevated lactate despite
adequate fluid resuscitation.

SCREENING TOOLS
The quick SOFA (qSOFA) score uses altered mental status, respiratory rate
>=22/min, and systolic blood pressure <=100 mmHg to identify patients at
risk outside the intensive care unit.

INITIAL MANAGEMENT (HOUR-1 BUNDLE)
Recommended initial interventions include measuring lactate level, obtaining
blood cultures prior to antibiotic administration, administering
broad-spectrum antibiotics, initiating rapid administration of 30 mL/kg
crystalloid fluid for hypotension or lactate >=4 mmol/L, and applying
vasopressors if hypotension persists despite fluid resuscitation to maintain
mean arterial pressure >=65 mmHg.

SOURCE CONTROL
Identifying and controlling the source of infection, such as drainage of an
abscess or removal of infected devices, is critical alongside antimicrobial
therapy.

MONITORING
Ongoing monitoring includes serial lactate measurements, urine output,
mental status, and hemodynamic parameters to guide resuscitation.

ANTIBIOTIC DE-ESCALATION
Once culture results are available, antibiotic therapy should be narrowed
based on identified organism and susceptibility patterns to reduce
resistance development and adverse effects.

PROGNOSIS
Early recognition and prompt initiation of the treatment bundle are
strongly associated with improved survival outcomes in sepsis and septic
shock.

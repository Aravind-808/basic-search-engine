TITLE: Acute Stroke Management Protocol

OVERVIEW
Stroke is a medical emergency resulting from disrupted blood supply to the
brain, classified as ischemic (approximately 87 percent of cases) or
hemorrhagic.

RECOGNITION
The FAST mnemonic (Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to
call emergency services) is widely used for public recognition of stroke
symptoms. Additional signs include sudden numbness, confusion, vision
changes, difficulty walking, and severe headache.

INITIAL ASSESSMENT
Rapid assessment includes determining time of symptom onset (or last known
well), obtaining vital signs, performing a National Institutes of Health
Stroke Scale (NIHSS) assessment, and urgent noncontrast head CT to
distinguish ischemic from hemorrhagic stroke.

ISCHEMIC STROKE MANAGEMENT
Intravenous thrombolysis with alteplase or tenecteplase is considered for
eligible patients presenting within the therapeutic window, typically 4.5
hours from symptom onset, after excluding contraindications such as active
bleeding or recent surgery. Mechanical thrombectomy may be performed for
large vessel occlusions within an extended window in appropriately selected
patients.

HEMORRHAGIC STROKE MANAGEMENT
Management focuses on blood pressure control, reversal of anticoagulation if
applicable, and neurosurgical consultation for possible evacuation depending
on hematoma size and location.

SECONDARY PREVENTION
Post-stroke management includes antiplatelet or anticoagulant therapy
depending on stroke etiology, statin therapy, blood pressure control, and
risk factor modification including smoking cessation and diabetes
management.

REHABILITATION
Multidisciplinary rehabilitation involving physical, occupational, and
speech therapy is critical for functional recovery following stroke.

TIME METRICS
Door-to-needle time targets aim for thrombolytic administration within 60
minutes of hospital arrival to minimize disability from ischemic stroke.

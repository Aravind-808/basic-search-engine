TITLE: Pediatric Vaccination Schedule - Overview

OVERVIEW
Routine childhood immunization protects against numerous infectious
diseases and is a cornerstone of preventive pediatric care.

BIRTH TO 15 MONTHS
Recommended vaccines during this period typically include hepatitis B,
rotavirus, diphtheria-tetanus-acellular pertussis (DTaP), Haemophilus
influenzae type b (Hib), pneumococcal conjugate vaccine (PCV), inactivated
poliovirus vaccine (IPV), influenza (annual, starting at 6 months), measles-
mumps-rubella (MMR), varicella, and hepatitis A.

18 MONTHS TO 6 YEARS
Booster doses of DTaP, IPV, MMR, and varicella are administered, along with
continued annual influenza vaccination.

ADOLESCENT SCHEDULE
Recommended adolescent vaccines include tetanus-diphtheria-pertussis (Tdap)
booster, human papillomavirus (HPV) vaccine series, and meningococcal
conjugate vaccine, with a booster dose typically given around age 16.

CATCH-UP IMMUNIZATION
Catch-up schedules are available for children and adolescents who have
fallen behind on recommended vaccines, allowing appropriate spacing based on
minimum intervals between doses.

CONTRAINDICATIONS AND PRECAUTIONS
Severe allergic reaction to a previous dose or vaccine component is a
contraindication. Moderate to severe acute illness generally warrants
postponement, while mild illness is not a contraindication.

ADVERSE EVENTS
Most vaccine reactions are mild, including injection site soreness, low-
grade fever, and fussiness. Serious adverse events are rare and are
monitored through post-marketing surveillance systems.

IMPORTANCE OF HERD IMMUNITY
High vaccination coverage protects vulnerable populations who cannot be
vaccinated, including infants too young for certain vaccines and
immunocompromised individuals.

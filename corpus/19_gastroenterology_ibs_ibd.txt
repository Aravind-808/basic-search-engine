TITLE: Irritable Bowel Syndrome and Inflammatory Bowel Disease

OVERVIEW
Irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) and inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) are
distinct gastrointestinal conditions that share overlapping symptoms but
differ substantially in pathophysiology and management.

IRRITABLE BOWEL SYNDROME
IBS is a functional gastrointestinal disorder characterized by recurrent
abdominal pain associated with altered bowel habits, in the absence of
identifiable structural or biochemical abnormalities. Subtypes include
IBS with constipation, IBS with diarrhea, and mixed type. Diagnosis relies
on symptom-based criteria such as the Rome IV criteria, with limited testing
to exclude alarm features.

IBS MANAGEMENT
Management includes dietary modification such as a low-FODMAP diet, fiber
supplementation, antispasmodics, and in some cases, low-dose tricyclic
antidepressants or gut-directed psychotherapy for symptom control.

INFLAMMATORY BOWEL DISEASE
IBD encompasses Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis, both characterized
by chronic immune-mediated intestinal inflammation. Crohn's disease can
affect any part of the gastrointestinal tract in a discontinuous pattern
with transmural inflammation, while ulcerative colitis affects the colon
continuously, beginning in the rectum, with inflammation limited to the
mucosa.

IBD SYMPTOMS
Common symptoms include chronic diarrhea, abdominal pain, rectal bleeding
(more common in ulcerative colitis), weight loss, and fatigue. Extraintestinal
manifestations may involve joints, skin, eyes, and the hepatobiliary system.

DIAGNOSIS OF IBD
Diagnosis involves endoscopy with biopsy, cross-sectional imaging, and
laboratory markers such as fecal calprotectin, C-reactive protein, and
erythrocyte sedimentation rate.

IBD TREATMENT
Treatment options include 5-aminosalicylates, corticosteroids for acute
flares, immunomodulators such as azathioprine, and biologic agents
targeting TNF-alpha, integrins, or interleukins. Surgical intervention may
be required for complications such as strictures, fistulas, or medically
refractory disease.

DISTINGUISHING FEATURES
Unlike IBS, IBD is associated with objective findings of inflammation,
increased risk of colorectal cancer with long-standing disease, and
potential for serious structural complications.

TITLE: Cancer Screening Guidelines - Overview

OVERVIEW
Cancer screening aims to detect malignancy at an early, more treatable
stage in asymptomatic individuals, reducing cancer-specific mortality.

BREAST CANCER
Mammography screening is generally recommended biennially for women aged
50-74, with earlier or more frequent screening considered for those with
elevated risk based on family history or genetic mutations such as BRCA1/2.

CERVICAL CANCER
Screening with Papanicolaou (Pap) smear and/or high-risk HPV testing is
recommended starting at age 21, with intervals depending on age and test
type, typically every 3-5 years.

COLORECTAL CANCER
Screening options include colonoscopy every 10 years, fecal immunochemical
testing (FIT) annually, or stool DNA testing every 1-3 years, generally
beginning at age 45 for average-risk individuals.

LUNG CANCER
Low-dose CT screening is recommended annually for adults aged 50-80 with a
significant smoking history (20 pack-years) who currently smoke or have
quit within the past 15 years.

PROSTATE CANCER
Prostate-specific antigen (PSA) testing is considered on an individualized
basis after shared decision-making, given the balance of potential benefits
and harms including overdiagnosis.

RISK-BASED SCREENING
Individuals with hereditary cancer syndromes, such as Lynch syndrome or
BRCA mutations, often require earlier initiation and more frequent
surveillance across multiple cancer types.

LIMITATIONS
Screening programs carry risks of false positives, overdiagnosis, and
procedure-related complications, underscoring the importance of shared
decision-making between patients and clinicians.

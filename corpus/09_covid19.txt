TITLE: COVID-19 - Clinical Features and Management

OVERVIEW
COVID-19 is caused by SARS-CoV-2, a novel coronavirus first identified in
late 2019 that produces a spectrum of illness ranging from asymptomatic
infection to severe respiratory failure.

TRANSMISSION
The virus spreads primarily via respiratory droplets and aerosols, with
close contact and poorly ventilated indoor spaces increasing transmission
risk.

CLINICAL PRESENTATION
Common symptoms include fever, cough, fatigue, myalgia, sore throat, loss of
taste or smell, and dyspnea. Severe cases can progress to acute respiratory
distress syndrome (ARDS), multi-organ dysfunction, and thromboembolic
complications.

RISK FACTORS FOR SEVERE DISEASE
Older age, obesity, diabetes, cardiovascular disease, chronic lung disease,
and immunosuppression are associated with increased risk of severe illness
and hospitalization.

DIAGNOSIS
Diagnosis relies on RT-PCR or antigen testing of nasopharyngeal or nasal
swab specimens. Chest imaging may show bilateral ground-glass opacities in
moderate to severe cases.

MANAGEMENT
Mild cases are managed with supportive care including rest, hydration, and
antipyretics. Hospitalized patients with hypoxemia may benefit from
supplemental oxygen, corticosteroids such as dexamethasone, and antiviral
agents. Monoclonal antibodies and antivirals may be used in high-risk
outpatients when appropriate.

PREVENTION
Vaccination significantly reduces risk of severe disease and hospitalization.
Additional preventive measures include hand hygiene, ventilation
improvements, and masking in high-risk settings.

LONG-TERM SEQUELAE
A subset of patients experience persistent symptoms including fatigue,
cognitive difficulties, and dyspnea beyond the acute illness, sometimes
referred to as post-COVID condition.

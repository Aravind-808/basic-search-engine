TITLE: Cardiovascular Disease Risk Factors and Prevention

OVERVIEW
Cardiovascular disease (CVD) remains a leading cause of death globally,
encompassing coronary artery disease, stroke, peripheral artery disease, and
heart failure.

MODIFIABLE RISK FACTORS
Key modifiable risk factors include hypertension, dyslipidemia, smoking,
diabetes mellitus, obesity, physical inactivity, and unhealthy diet high in
saturated fats and sodium. Chronic stress and poor sleep quality are
increasingly recognized contributors.

NON-MODIFIABLE RISK FACTORS
Age, sex, and family history of premature cardiovascular disease are
non-modifiable factors that inform overall risk stratification.

RISK ASSESSMENT TOOLS
Clinicians commonly use risk calculators such as the Pooled Cohort
Equations or the Framingham Risk Score to estimate 10-year cardiovascular
risk based on age, cholesterol levels, blood pressure, smoking status, and
diabetes status.

LIPID MANAGEMENT
LDL cholesterol is a primary target for intervention. Statins remain first
line for lipid-lowering therapy, with additional agents such as ezetimibe or
PCSK9 inhibitors used in high-risk or statin-intolerant patients.

LIFESTYLE MODIFICATION
The American Heart Association recommends at least 150 minutes of
moderate-intensity aerobic activity per week, a diet rich in fruits,
vegetables, whole grains, and lean protein, smoking cessation, and
maintenance of a healthy body weight.

PHARMACOLOGIC PREVENTION
Low-dose aspirin may be considered for secondary prevention in patients with
established cardiovascular disease. Antihypertensive and glucose-lowering
therapies also reduce cardiovascular risk when comorbid conditions are
present.

SCREENING
Routine screening includes blood pressure checks, lipid panels starting in
adulthood, and diabetes screening in at-risk populations.
